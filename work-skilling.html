    /01 Project Overview

    Crafting meaningful learning for blue-collar workers.

    Two years ago, Oust became part of BetterPlace, but it remained a separate product. BetterPlace has since rebranded as GoBetter HRMS solutions, and Oust, as an individual product, was discontinued. Instead, it merged into GoBetter as Up-skill.

    As part of that change, we completely redesigned the app. The revamped experience is more engaging, meaningful, and aspiring for frontline workers, and it helps the business grow, improves client satisfaction, and increases productivity and retention.

    /02 Context and Users

    Built for the people who keep industries moving.

    Oust set out to help blue-collar workers across India improve their skills. It offers courses, assessments, personalised playlists and even contests to make learning more interesting, in over 35 languages including the major Indian ones.

    Organisations in blue-collar industries use it to train employees on job-related topics: up-skilling, compliance and work processes, career development and re-skilling. In turn, it helps workers keep learning and growing, and opens new chances in their careers.

    /03 Why Redesign

    Three reasons the old app had to change.

    01
    Integrating Oust with GoBetter
    As BetterPlace rebranded into GoBetter, Oust stopped being a separate product. It became part of GoBetter, called Up-skill.
    02
    Expanding into various geographies
    As GoBetter expands internationally and looks for product-market fit there, a uniform, hassle-free experience for end users becomes essential.
    03
    Client dissatisfaction
    Customers asked for improvements. After the integration, none were made and the experience stayed as it was.

    /04 Challenges

    The same friction, seen from two sides.

    Before designing anything, we mapped what wasn't working for the organisations that assign learning, and for the workers who have to complete it.

    Faced by organisations

    01
    Low course completion rates

    Organisations struggle to make sure employees finish courses on time. Average completion sits at 13–15%, which is well short of what they need.

    02
    Unable to prioritise content by importance

    Admins couldn't push important modules forward, so end users didn't discover them and admins had to chase completion manually.

    03
    Challenges with content creation

    Without structure, format or guided navigation, admins found features and configurations hard to use, especially first-time users.

    04
    Low employee engagement in the app

    An outdated look and an unclear state left users with little idea of what to do, when, and why, so they engaged less.

    05
    Customisation concerns

    Admins could freely change backgrounds, CTAs and colours, but that freedom didn't protect brand identity, and it made the experience inconsistent for employees.

    06
    No web version of the app

    When modules are assigned only every few months, users hesitate to download the app, and admins would rather share a link that works on the web.

    Faced by users

    01
    Little motivation to complete assigned modules

    A cluttered, outdated UI, weak content and no decision-making guidance drained motivation to engage.

    02
    Critical modules stay out of sight

    Nothing surfaced on the landing page. Users had to dig through My Learning, where modules were sorted by date rather than urgency.

    03
    No visibility into ongoing learning

    Coming back to resume a module meant hunting through the Learn listing and applying filters, which made users lose interest.

    04
    Confusing navigation

    Events and mandatory items were hard to find. New users in particular searched the calendar and still missed them.

    05
    Module types are hard to tell apart

    With no visual identity separating a course, an assessment and a playlist, users couldn't decide what to start first.

    06
    No guidance while performing tasks

    Assessments felt like filling out a form, with no clear instruction per question type and no hints along the way.

    07
    Inconsistent, unexpected UI

    Heavy admin customisation kept changing CTA types and colours, so it was never clear how to interact with the app.

    08
    Lengthy, low-engagement content

    Users had limited time, and playlists that felt never-ending made completing them difficult.

    /05 Scope of Intervention

    Eight places to intervene.

    Those challenges gave us a clear brief. These are the areas we chose to focus on.

    01
    An action-focused home

    A dynamic homepage that treats each user intelligently by serving the critical things they need to do.

    02
    Distinct module types

    Learning modules that look different, so each one clearly defines its purpose in the learning journey.

    03
    Playful assessments

    An assessment experience that is more engaging and fun for the people taking it.

    04
    Clear, linear navigation

    Simple and intuitive, with guided navigation where workflows get complex.

    05
    Flexible theming

    A wide variety of theming that is contextual, effortless, and blends with each organisation's brand.

    06
    Encouraging feedback

    A feedback mechanism that is efficient, visual, informative and encouraging.

    07
    Short courses

    Bite-sized content lets users engage quickly and grasp it fast, leading to faster completion.

    08
    Recommendations

    Suggesting what to complete first or next removes ambiguity and speeds up task completion.

    /06 Process

    Wireframes first, then the UI.

    We wireframed extensively before moving to UI. The wireframes helped us visualise and finalise the flow and design direction, gather feedback from leadership and stakeholders, and align on the design direction. A well-planned, chapter-by-chapter approach to the revamp cleared up confusion and built clarity early.

    Design principles

    Setting principles early kept us focused on our objectives and made sure the designs matched the outcomes we wanted.

    One at a timeMedia-first approachAction focusedInstructiveBuilding association & familiarityEfficient feedback and progressGuided and personalised experience

    User flow

    We kept the main flow structure unchanged because of the existing backend architecture, but the principles we followed to restructure the homepage shaped the whole product. Navigation got simpler, a module launcher on the home page gives easy access to every module, and learning progress sits on top for visibility.

    Users mostly opened the app to finish pending modules. They landed on the homepage and then went to the Learn tab to complete them, and feeds only added friction along the way. So we moved feeds into a tab of their own and let the homepage focus on learning.

    Events were previously reachable only through the calendar, which users struggled to discover. They now have a distinct entry point on the homepage, with every event listed in one place.

    /07 Designing the Home

    Making learning frontline friendly.

    The home page helps you complete tasks with clear guidance and easy access to everything you need. Our aim was to help users find what they need, keep track of assigned and completed modules, and understand which tasks need attention, all from one screen.

    01
    Task-oriented, contextual content

    Whatever the tasks users need to accomplish will be on top, in priority order.

    02
    Instructive

    The homepage guides users through specific tasks by prioritising clarity, with efficient visual cues, a prominent CTA and strong visual hierarchy.

    03
    Quick access

    Clear, direct navigation to every independent module.

    04
    Learning progress indicator

    Overall progress on top gives users a view of their own learning path and encourages them to finish what remains.

    /08 Course-Based Learning

    Familiar controls. Clearer paths.

    A course is a lesson type that can hold multiple chapters, built from videos, images, audio transcripts, or a mix of them. Play controls and CTAs are common across learning apps, and our users mostly interact with video, so we kept them close to what people already know.

    01
    Promoting pending courses

    A visual treatment and a running count-down capture attention and help users complete pending courses on time.

    02
    Set of courses

    When several courses are assigned at once, one is highlighted in the banner and the rest stack as a bundle, so nothing overwhelms the user.

    03
    Quick start

    A prominent play button starts learning quickly, while View details is there for those who want to know more first.

    04
    Scalable structure

    The card has a clear hierarchy. Badges, coins and social proof influence the decision to start, and length, chapters and topics are visible up front.

    Consuming a course

    Learning via video

    Because our users mostly interact with video-based content, the player keeps the familiar controls they already know.

    Text-based course with audio playback

    Text content can come with or without audio transcripts. A transcript helps users consume the content effortlessly.

    Visual indicators

    Indicators now communicate any mandatory watch time assigned to a piece of content. While a mandatory time is running, the control actions stay locked for the user.

    Collecting user sentiment

    Capturing learning experiences helps us understand the effectiveness and relevance of the delivered content. We simplified the feedback process so that sharing an experience takes just a tap.

    /09 Assessments

    Assessments that feel less like forms.

    Assessments support a wide range of question types and give engaging feedback on every response. The old experience felt like filling out a form, with no clear instruction for each question type, so we rebuilt it to be playful and instructive.

    Crafting consistency

    The assessment card is designed around the presence or absence of a banner image, which is normally an inconsistent element across assessments. A set of defined patterns and a contextual colour-picking mechanism keep it visually consistent and clean.

    Theming brand experience with ease

    Letting organisations align the app with their brand is a key selling point, but excessive customisation and a lack of branding sense among admins had become a hygiene issue. Admins now choose a colour from a predefined palette that fits their brand or the subject's context, and a set of dynamic patterns keeps assessments from feeling like form filling, without affecting the product's visual hygiene.

    A playful assessment experience

    Each assessment is structured to support the range of question types while making sure every response is captured intuitively.

    Responses with audio, video and image

    Some questions ask for more than a tap. Users can record audio, capture video or upload an image, preview what they've recorded before submitting, and record again if they need to.

    /10 Playlists and Progress

    A bundle of lessons, made finishable.

    A playlist combines courses and assessments for a specific learning purpose. It can hold multiple levels, each either a course or an assessment, based on admin configuration. Playlists take more time to complete, since they bundle several courses and assessments and can run long.

    The playlist card

    The card gives a clear overview of the playlist's characteristics and purpose, visually indicating that it contains multiple lesson modules. The top card changes dynamically with the sequence, giving the user real-time visibility, showing the number of levels, and navigating seamlessly to the level page.

    Anatomy of the playlist

    The cover area gives a prominent overview of the playlist with minimal distraction, and a dynamic guiding CTA shows progress along the lesson path. Below it, levels are listed in sequence and each one nests multiple chapters. By default only one level is expanded, so users can focus on that content and see their progress within it.

    Celebrating every small achievement

    Concise feedback when users complete chapters, levels or playlists helps maintain their engagement and motivation. These moments act as checkpoints that drive engagement and progress.

    My learning

    A catalogue of the user's learning journey

    My Learning is a central hub for browsing and accessing everything assigned to a user, organised so it's easy to find their way. It has a clear entry point from the quick access area of the home page. The top gives an overview of progress and achievements, and below it pending modules are categorised by type, with visual indicators for the criticality and status of each.

    /11 Events and Micro-learning

    Beyond the course: live and bite-sized.

    Two more ways to learn rounded out the experience. Live, instructor-led events bring a classroom into the app, and stories-style micro-courses fit learning into the short gaps of a shift.

    Instructor-led

    Training using live classes and events

    Live classes bring real-time instruction and interaction, like a classroom, but in a virtual setting. Events can also take place in a physical location. Although courses, assessments and playlists are asynchronous, events add a blended learning approach where trainers and learners interact directly.

    Event cards can appear on the homepage in three cases: to register for a new event, for an already registered event, and when an event is currently live. Visibility of upcoming and registered events stops users missing them, and the card's state, visual cues and CTA change to match it.

    Micro-learning

    Stories-style mini courses

    To boost engagement and address challenges like short attention spans, we're testing stories-style micro-courses for a few select industries.

    Short courses

    Short courses are bite-sized learning that cater to busy schedules and shrinking attention spans. Breaking down complex topics into digestible segments makes the learning faster and easier, and a short quiz at the end lets users check that they've grasped it.
