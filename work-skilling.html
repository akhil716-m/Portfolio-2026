      Up-skill

      A ground-up redesign of GoBetter's learning app for blue-collar workers, built around one question: what should I do next?

      Product Designer
      GoBetter (formerly BetterPlace)
      3 months, launched April 2024

      Crafting meaningful learning for blue-collar workers.

      Oust became part of BetterPlace but stayed a separate product. When BetterPlace rebranded as GoBetter, Oust was retired and folded in as Up-skill.

      We redesigned the app completely. The new experience is more engaging and aspiring for frontline workers, and helps the business grow through client satisfaction, productivity and retention.

      Product designer
      Showing only the information that helps users act directly from the home page, with more emphasis on CTAs and engaging elements like coins and badges.

      Working with Bijoy Prasad Shah and Sugata Biswas.

      The problem

      Completion sat at 13-15%. Workers couldn't see what mattered, and admins couldn't guide them.

      Built for the people who keep industries moving.

      Oust helps blue-collar workers across India build skills through courses, assessments, playlists and contests, in over 35 languages.

      Organisations use it to train teams on up-skilling, compliance, work processes and career development, so workers keep learning and open new career paths.

      Three reasons the old app had to change.

      Integrating Oust with GoBetter

      As BetterPlace rebranded into GoBetter, Oust stopped being a separate product. It became part of GoBetter, called Up-skill.

      Expanding into various geographies

      As GoBetter expands internationally and looks for product-market fit there, a uniform, hassle-free experience for end users becomes essential.

      Client dissatisfaction

      Customers asked for improvements. After the integration, none were made and the experience stayed as it was.

      A feed-first home with five tabs became a task-first home: progress on top, quick access to every module, then the next course.

      A dense player with tiny controls became full-screen media with chapter progress, coins and one row of controls.

      A bare confirmation with a red “Over!” button became feedback that rewards effort and points to what comes next.

      Where the journey kept breaking.

      Fourteen problems across two audiences: the admins who assign courses and the workers who are meant to finish them.

      13-15%

      average course completion

      Admins 6 pain points

      I couldn't push what mattered, so I chased completion by hand.

      • Low course completion rates

        Average completion sits at 13-15%, well short of what organisations need.

      • Unable to prioritise content by importance

        Important modules couldn't be pushed, so admins chased completion by hand.

      • Challenges with content creation

        No structure or guided navigation made features hard to use, especially the first time.

      • Low employee engagement in the app

        An outdated look and unclear state left users unsure what to do, and why.

      • Customisation concerns

        Free colour and CTA changes didn't protect brand identity, and felt inconsistent to employees.

      • No web version of the app

        With modules assigned every few months, users hesitate to download an app.

      Frontline workers 8 pain points

      Nothing told me what to do next, so I stopped.

      • Little motivation to complete assigned modules

        A cluttered, outdated UI and no guidance on what to pick drained motivation.

      • Critical modules stay out of sight

        Nothing surfaced on the landing page; My Learning sorted by date, not urgency.

      • No visibility into ongoing learning

        Resuming a module meant hunting through the Learn listing and filters.

      • Confusing navigation

        Events and mandatory items were hard to find, even in the calendar.

      • Module types are hard to tell apart

        A course, an assessment and a playlist looked alike, so nobody knew what to start first.

      • No guidance while performing tasks

        Assessments felt like forms, with no hints along the way.

      • Inconsistent, unexpected UI

        Heavy customisation kept changing CTAs and colours, so it was never clear how to interact.

      • Lengthy, low-engagement content

        Users had little time, and never-ending playlists made finishing hard.

      The approach

      Wireframe first, set principles early, then rebuild the flow chapter by chapter.

      Wireframes first, then the UI.

      We wireframed before designing UI, chapter by chapter, to settle the flow and get leadership and stakeholder feedback early.

      Design principles

      Setting principles early kept every design decision tied to the outcomes we wanted.

      One at a timeMedia-first approachAction focusedInstructiveBuilding association & familiarityEfficient feedback and progressGuided and personalised experience

      User flow

      The backend limited us, so we kept the main flow and restructured the homepage around simpler navigation: a module launcher for easy access, and learning progress on top.

      Users mostly came to finish pending modules, and feeds only added friction on the way. Feeds now have their own tab, and the homepage focuses on learning.

      Events were hard to find in the calendar. They now have a distinct homepage entry point, with every event listed in one place.

      The product

      A home screen that says what to do next, then courses, assessments and playlists built around it.

      Making learning frontline friendly.

      One screen to find what you need, track assigned and completed modules, and see which tasks need attention.

      1. Learning progress indicator

        Overall progress sits on top. It gives users a view of their own learning path and encourages them to finish what remains.

      2. Quick access

        Clear, direct navigation to every independent module: mini courses, courses, assessments and playlists.

      3. Task-oriented, contextual content

        Whatever users need to accomplish is on top, in priority order, so the next task is always the first thing they see.

      4. Instructive

        The homepage guides users through specific tasks with clear visual cues, a prominent CTA and a strong visual hierarchy.

      Familiar controls. Clearer paths.

      A course holds multiple chapters of video, images or audio transcripts. Users mostly watch video, so we kept familiar controls and CTAs.

      Promoting pending courses
      A visual treatment and a running count-down capture attention and help users complete pending courses on time.
      Set of courses
      When several courses are assigned at once, one is highlighted in the banner and the rest stack as a bundle, so nothing overwhelms the user.
      Quick start
      A prominent play button starts learning quickly, while View details is there for those who want to know more first.
      Scalable structure
      The card has a clear hierarchy. Badges, coins and social proof influence the decision to start, and length, chapters and topics are visible up front.

      One course, four ways to take it in

      Learning via video

      Video is full-screen by default. Tap to bring up the controls, chapter progress and coins, and open the description over the video.

      Text with audio

      Text courses can come with or without an audio transcript, so users can listen instead of reading.

      Mandatory watch time

      Indicators show any mandatory watch time. While it is running, the control actions stay locked.

      One-tap feedback

      Capturing how a course landed helps judge its effectiveness and relevance, so rating it takes a tap.

      Assessments that feel less like forms.

      The old assessments felt like filling out a form. We rebuilt them to be playful, instructive and engaging on every question type.

      Crafting consistency

      Assessment cards vary by whether they carry a banner image, which made them inconsistent. Defined patterns and a contextual colour picker keep them clean and consistent.

      Theming brand experience with ease

      Brand alignment is a key selling point, but unrestrained customisation had become a hygiene issue. Admins now pick from a predefined palette, and dynamic patterns keep assessments from feeling like form filling.

      A playful assessment experience

      Each assessment supports the full range of question types and captures every response intuitively.

      Try a theme

      Same question, three brands

      Admins pick a colour from a predefined palette, and a pattern comes with it. Playful and on-brand, without touching the product's visual hygiene.

      Responses with audio, video and image

      Some questions need more than a tap. Users can record audio, capture video or upload an image, preview it before submitting, and record again.

      A bundle of lessons, made finishable.

      A playlist bundles courses and assessments into levels for one learning purpose, configured by the admin. Because it bundles so much, it can run long.

      The playlist card

      The card shows a playlist's purpose and that it holds several lessons. The top card updates with the sequence and links straight to the current level.

      Anatomy of the playlist

      The cover gives a clear overview with minimal distraction, and a guiding CTA shows progress. Levels below are listed in order and nest their chapters, with one expanded by default.

      Celebrating every small achievement

      Concise feedback when users complete chapters, levels or playlists keeps them motivated and marks their progress.

      A catalogue of the user's learning journey

      My Learning is the hub for everything assigned, reached from quick access on the home page. Progress sits on top, with pending modules grouped by type and marked by criticality and status.

      Beyond the course: live and bite-sized.

      Two more ways to learn: live events that bring the classroom into the app, and stories-style micro-courses for short gaps in a shift.

      Training using live classes and events

      Live classes bring real-time instruction and interaction, in a virtual setting or a physical location. Unlike courses, assessments and playlists, they let trainers and learners interact directly.

      Event cards appear on the homepage for new, registered and live events, and their state, cues and CTA change to match, so users don't miss them.

      Stories-style mini courses

      To boost engagement and fight short attention spans, we're testing stories-style micro-courses for a few select industries.

      Short courses

      Short courses are bite-sized learning for busy schedules. Complex topics arrive in digestible segments, and a short quiz at the end checks understanding.
